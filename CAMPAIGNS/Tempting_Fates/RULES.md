# Tempting Fates: mortal rules 0.1.0

This is a complete **level-one tabletop opening** for a human referee and one to six travelers. Bring d4, d6, d8 and d20 dice, a character sheet, a shared map and a supply record. The adapted core comes from Ben Milton / Questing Beast's *Knave 1.0*, CC BY 4.0; see [attribution and changes](ATTRIBUTION.md). The Greek setting, equipment, prices, travel procedures and restrictions below are this campaign's original choices, not historical claims.

The tone is **dark tragic mythology: horror, hubris and divine punishment**. Mortals are vulnerable; power and a successful roll do not guarantee safety. A god's particular demands can conflict with another's, and people can judge the same deed differently. Make a dangerous taboo discoverable and its immediate stakes clear; do not replace those conflicts with a hidden universal morality score or treat the Fates as judges of virtue.

The referee records HP, gear quantities, travel and projects on paper or a separate tabletop record. The seven [rules calculators](../../scenarios/tempted-fates/campaign/rules.ts) return previews from explicit dice. They do not roll, prove consent, change an ARGO world, or create an event. ARGO's existing signed custody, speech and informed time-transfer actions are a separate authority surface. Tabletop death is **not** a call to Atropos's `end_thread`, and advancing a clock does not automatically debit a character's lifetime. Reconcile any future combat integration through a reviewed causal-rules extension.

## Make a traveler

1. Name a mortal and something they were doing before this expedition. Choose a craft, a current obligation, one priority, and a limit they will not cross. These give context, not a class or automatic permission. There is no alignment, global morality, reputation or degeneracy score. Others retain their own impressions of you.
2. In this order, roll three d6 for Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma. The **lowest** die is that ability's bonus; its defense is bonus +10. For example, 6,2,5 gives +2 and defense 12. After all six, you may exchange the complete bonus/defense pair of two abilities once.
3. Roll **1d8 once** for maximum HP and start at that HP. There is no minimum, reroll or Constitution addition. A character with 1HP should consider help, distance and retreat. Start at level one.
4. Take ordinary clothes, sandals, cloak and a carrying sack; one knife, staff, spear or sling; two daily rations; two full waterskins; a lamp; one measure of lamp oil; a fire kit; and **20 obols**. The free kit is a fixed campaign starting allowance, not a repeatable reward. Record each possession and its slots using the tables below. There is no free armor. Purchase anything else that a willing seller actually has.
5. Capacity is Constitution **defense**, not its bonus. Check carried slots before leaving. Record HP, supplies, party members, the departure time, expected return checkpoint, and who knows any private information. Each player controls only their own traveler.

The basic kit occupies 9 slots with a knife, staff or sling, or 10 with a spear; even Constitution defense 11 leaves room for a small purchase. Loaded water containers and remaining coins count. Ordinary clothing, cloak and the empty carrying sack take zero slots; hiding objects in them grants no extra capacity.

| Ability | Common uses |
| --- | --- |
| Strength | Melee attacks, lifting, forcing a stuck door, holding a line |
| Dexterity | Balance, quiet movement, climbing, evasion |
| Constitution | Exposure, poison, endurance; capacity and ordinary healing |
| Intelligence | Careful craft, mechanisms, reading unfamiliar marks, recalling learned lore |
| Wisdom | Ranged attacks, observation, tracking, navigation |
| Charisma | Making a case, bargaining, misleading, calming an uncertain listener |

A background explains ordinary competence: a boatwright can inspect a familiar hull without a roll. It does not grant unexplained knowledge, guaranteed success, an extra ability bonus or authority over another person's decision.

## Say what you try; roll only for uncertainty

The referee describes what this traveler can perceive, asks what they intend, and states the relevant danger and likely cost before a consequential roll. Ask for details if the method is unclear. A safe feasible task succeeds without dice; an impossible one needs a different method. An obviously visible clue is available without a perception toll. Hidden information requires an actual opportunity to observe or investigate.

For an uncertain task with consequences, roll d20 + the relevant bonus. **A total greater than 15 succeeds. A tie fails.** Dexterity +2 with a 13 gives 15 and fails; a 14 gives 16 and succeeds. Natural 1 and 20 have no additional automatic effect in this adaptation. If another person's active resistance is the uncertainty, the acting side rolls once against that person's relevant ability defense instead of 15; a tie still favors the defender. Do not roll again from the other side.

Useful preparation or a decisive tactical benefit grants advantage: roll two d20 and keep the higher. Serious interference grants disadvantage: keep the lower. Multiple benefits do not add dice; advantage and disadvantage together cancel to one normal roll. State the mode before rolling. Assistance requires a plausible method and puts the helper within the announced risks; it can supply advantage, not a pile of bonuses.

Failure produces the announced consequence, such as noise, a broken tool, lost time, a worse position or injury. It need not erase an already discovered fact. Another attempt requires changed circumstances or paying the declared additional cost; repeated free rolls cannot grind a barrier into success. For an unknown ruling, the referee states a provisional ability, target and stakes, hears objections, then records the ruling for consistency.

A Charisma success can obtain a hearing or a better ordinary offer. It cannot dictate another player's choice, make an NPC violate an established refusal, manufacture a witness, or supply consent to a time gift. Informed gifts require the donor's independent, explicit, revocable authorization through the actual consent procedure. Neither charm, an oath, a rumor nor a good roll substitutes for it.

Prices include simple containers where needed. Only the explicit bundles above share a slot. An empty oil vessel still occupies its bundle space until discarded or refilled; a consumed ration frees its slot. No nested sack creates capacity. Carrying above capacity is reported by the tool; at the table the traveler must leave, cache, hand over or abandon identified possessions before ordinary travel or combat movement. Emergency dragging is a slow, noisy task adjudicated openly, not an unlimited inventory exemption. A carried casualty uses both hands and six extra slots; if that exceeds capacity, two bearers may divide the load into three slots each, moving at half speed.

Armor does not subtract damage. Wear at most one armor suit; choose its defense, add a readied shield if possible, and otherwise use unarmored defense **11**. There is no extra Dexterity armor bonus, helmet bonus, stacking shield benefit, weapon-quality clock or critical-hit damage in this release. Repairs have agreed real material/labor costs, not automatic degradation on a natural 1. A loaned ordinary lamp's repair must be agreed and cannot exceed its 5-obol replacement price. A free loan does not imply a deposit, forced labor, or endless free oil.

## Exploration, time and supplies

The campaign calendar is synthetic: a **round is 6 seconds**, an **exploration turn 600 seconds**, a **watch 4 hours**, a day 86,400 seconds, and a year 360 days. These are campaign choices. The referee keeps one elapsed-time record. Route lengths in the atlas are exploration turns; they are not secret distance rolls. A turn can cover a careful search of one small area, a substantial local task, or about 36m of cautious interior movement. Brief questions need not consume an entire turn; announce when a substantial task will.

At each turn: obtain intentions from everyone present, resolve compatible activities, advance elapsed time once, mark burning light, and announce newly observable changes. Someone studying a door does not stop everyone else's clock. Do not demand a secret-search roll merely to repeat the public room description. At a shared checkpoint, write who is present and what they actually tell one another; joining a party does not grant another actor's private memories.

Travel normally permits two watches (8 hours) of walking or rowing per day. Atlas travel times assume passable conditions and a suitable mode of transport. Rest, meals, waiting and camp fill the remaining time. Before attempting an additional travel watch, each traveler makes a Constitution check against 15; failure costs d4 HP at that watch's end. Apply this check to every extra watch and still charge food, water and elapsed time. Weather, darkness and an unsafe route can impose additional **announced** obstacles; a visible exit or turning back remains a choice.

Consume one ration and one waterskin allowance per traveler per 24 hours. Hot conditions or heavy exertion can require extra water if the referee declares the requirement before departure. Missing a day's food costs d4 HP at the daily checkpoint; missing its water costs a separate d4 HP. This damage can kill and is not prevented by calling it a rest. Refilling water needs an accessible suitable supply and any owner's permission. Food, burning fuel and ammunition cannot be recovered by resetting a scene. Used arrows/stones are not automatically recovered; the referee must identify recoverable ones before a search.

Foraging is an optional **four-hour watch**, not a free action. Before the attempt, the referee records a finite number of edible ration units actually available in the area. A Wisdom check against 15 gathers at most one of those units on success and removes it from that stock; failure spends the watch without a ration. Exhausted stock remains exhausted until an expressly adjudicated replenishment. Hunting, fishing and buying are alternative concrete undertakings, not promises of infinite supplies.

For rotating solo/party play, stop each expedition at a recorded safe location or agreed checkpoint with time, possessions and obligations. Resolve a shared location in one announced order. If two declared actions require the same object, settle custody before the second proceeds; pause a contested checkpoint for the referee. This manual procedure neither invents a fork nor rewrites an earlier acquisition time.

## Danger, combat and retreat

First describe the threat, visible armor, range and available exits. Combat is one possible consequence, not the default answer to disagreement. A clearly negotiated withdrawal needs no attack roll. If people actually fight, roll one d6 at the start of **each round**: 1–3 opposition acts first; 4–6 travelers act first. Everyone on that side may coordinate their order, but resolves each action once. Rerolling next round can let a side act twice across the boundary. This kit uses attacker rolls only, not the optional player-facing defense procedure from Knave.

On a turn, move up to **12m** and take one action: attack, attempt a stunt, use an item, help, or move another 12m. Melee reaches an adjacent opponent. A ranged attack needs clear sight, ammunition and freedom from an adjacent active enemy; use Wisdom bonus. Melee uses Strength. Roll d20 + the relevant bonus and **exceed the defender's armor defense**. Equality misses. On a hit roll the listed damage die and subtract that number of HP. No ability adds to damage. Unarmed striking deals 1 damage on a hit. A shot beyond the listed range and up to twice it has disadvantage; farther shots cannot hit through this procedure. A thrown spear leaves the thrower's possession and its landing place is recorded, hit or miss.

At positive HP a person stands; **at exactly 0 they are unconscious; below 0 they are dead** under these tabletop rules. Apply the actual damage without clamping at zero. An unconscious person cannot act and needs protection or transport; there is no automatic extra bleed-out roll. Dressing a wound does not restore HP. Rest can heal a surviving person at zero; it cannot resurrect someone below zero. State lethal risks before a stunt or strike rather than promising plot armor afterward. Ordinary injuries do not invoke Fate jurisdictions or the anomaly cutoff.

A stunt replaces an attack: name an attainable result such as disarming, blocking a passage, pushing back or escaping a grapple. The referee states which acting ability opposes which defense and what failure exposes. One strict check resolves it. A stunt does not also deal weapon damage. If a shove could cause a fall, disclose the fall's consequence first: use d6 damage per full 3m, up to 6d6, for this campaign; an effective rope or secure barrier may prevent the fall entirely. Advantage remains two dice; this release omits Knave's optional advantage-for-attack-plus-stunt exchange.

For a contested retreat, name an actual exit. On their turn a traveler can withdraw up to 12m using their action; that retreat itself grants no free enemy attack, but an enemy that later catches them may attack normally. Sprinting 24m uses the move plus action and may expose a declared route hazard. If pursuers and fugitives leave the immediate fight at equal speed, resolve **one** Dexterity check against the lead pursuer's Dexterity defense after a round of pursuit: success breaks contact; failure lets the pursuers close, or the fugitives can stop and parley. State how a wounded companion, choke point, dropped obstacle or different speeds changes that situation before the choice. Do not secretly teleport pursuers past a won escape.

These are original optional mortal templates, not new fields in seeded actors or a license to overwrite named NPC natures. Pick a justified template and visible equipment before conflict. The Fates have no combat stat block here, and players never inherit their jurisdictions. No bestiary or supernatural damage immunity is silently imported.

When opposition first loses a member, loses its leader, or has half its number defeated, check morale once for that trigger; a lone foe checks when first reduced to half its starting HP or less. Roll two d6: **a total at or below morale holds**; above it the referee chooses a credible retreat, surrender or parley, respecting the situation. An unreachable escape does not require suicide. Player characters choose their own courage; morale never controls them. A hireling facing withheld agreed pay or exceptional danger may likewise reconsider rather than become a compulsory shield.

## Particular gods, particular consequences

For a supernatural location, the human referee chooses and records its particular tradition and consequence **before** travelers test it. A public warning, witnessed example or informed local can reveal it. These optional original examples are manual situation rules, not extra facts installed in the authored world or an executable punishment engine. Use one only where the referee's existing location fiction supports it; they do not add a new ability to an ARGO actor.

| Patron and visible warning | Specific trigger | Bounded consequence and a way forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zeus of the accepted guest table: a lightning-split lintel and an explicit warning against bloodshed under this roof | Deliberately attack someone after accepting that house's hospitality; ordinary disagreement or leaving is not a trigger | A single thunderclap shatters one agreed piece of exposed crockery and bars this doorway with fallen timber. Spend one exploration turn clearing it with tools, succeed at a Strength check to force it sooner, or use an already described other exit. The trigger is then spent for this visit; it does not repeat each round or damage a character's lifespan. |
| Poseidon of a particular mooring: fresh flood marks show the exposed shoreline and a keeper warns against cutting its dedicated line | Knowingly cut that marked line while the water is rising | One surge reaches the warned shoreline. Everyone still in that area can retreat to the already identified high ground before the surge; someone who remains makes a Dexterity check against 15 or suffers d6 HP damage from debris. No hidden second wave is added. The wet route is unsafe for one watch; wait or take an actual alternative route. |
| Demeter of the winter store: the reserved baskets are visibly marked and their purpose explained | Knowingly consume grain from the specific store held for seed, without its custodian's agreement | One of the taker's carried edible rations spoils, if any is carried, and the visible mark on that store goes dark until the next dawn. No ration is conjured to be spoiled, and no HP is immediately removed. Replace what was taken by an ordinary consensual purchase/gift, negotiate, or leave bearing the shortage and people's own reactions. No compulsory vow is extracted. |

State these local stakes before the triggering choice. A person without access to the warning may encounter a warning first; ignorance is not permission for the referee to invent an unbounded punishment afterward. No temple's price, injury, intimidation or Charisma result becomes a donor's time-transfer consent. These finite episodes need not be fair or agreeable to their victims, but their procedures remain knowable and consistent. They never name the source scenario's anomaly, end a thread, guarantee the loom's appearance, or reveal ontology after a cutoff.

## Rest, projects and the limits of this release

Ordinary healing needs **eight uninterrupted hours** in a place safe enough to sleep, plus that day's food and water. Make at most one healing roll per traveler per 24 hours: d8 + Constitution bonus, added to current HP and capped at maximum. This campaign deliberately uses the same roll in a safe haven; it omits Knave's separate full-heal-at-haven benefit. A paid bed grants shelter, not food or extra rolls. Missing supplies, repeated naps, a vow or a successful social check cannot reset the daily healing allowance. Referee notes track when the next rest becomes available; `previewRest` only performs arithmetic.

Projects use a stated finite resource, a fixed positive number of segments, and an explicit trigger. At a checkpoint decide how many steps are actually supported by the work just done. Apply **the smallest of requested steps, available resources, and unfinished segments**; spend one resource per applied segment. For example, progress 1/6 with two resources and eight requested steps becomes 3/6 with zero resources. A completed project or one with no resources makes no further progress. Announce the published completion result once; do not recursively trigger another project, reroll a relic variant, or create fresh resources. Referee consequences and interruption choices must remain visible where characters can perceive them. This is an original bounded procedure inspired by the repository's clock research, not an autonomous tick engine.

This release supports **level one**; it contains no XP awards, ability advancement or automatic maximum-HP growth. Characters can continue multiple expeditions at that level, gaining actual tools, discovered routes, relationships, obligations and completed work. Agree a separate advancement module before introducing higher levels. A replacement traveler is a new individual, not a reset of the dead character's inventory or private knowledge; their kit enters once and their entry point must make sense.

Hospitality, burial, oaths and prayers can matter to people in this Greek-myth continuity. They do not confer automatic spells, fresh food, healing, resurrection, time gifts or divine obedience. There is no player spell list or executable ritual subsystem in this kit. Treat an offering as an actual finite possession surrendered voluntarily; any supernatural effect needs specific referee content and stated limits. Never improvise an effect that defeats the source scenario's informed choice, separate Fate roles, or post-anomaly silence. The present runtime's cutoff spans a world/branch, so ordinary fights, clock completion and failed checks must not manufacture an anomaly.

## Calculator reference

All inputs are closed passive data: extra fields, getters, proxies, malformed arrays, nonintegers, unsafe integers and negative zero fail with `INVALID_CONTRACT`. Returned objects are fresh. Dice must come from a real agreed table procedure or another trusted adjudicator; supplying a number does not prove a fair draw. None of these results appends an event.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `abilityFromDice` | Exactly three d6 | Lowest die bonus; defense bonus +10 |
| `resolveCheck` | One d20, or two in advantage/disadvantage; bonus 0–10; target | Selected die, total, strict `total > target` |
| `capacity` | Constitution defense 10–20; up to 1024 nonnegative slot costs | Occupied, possibly negative remaining, fit boolean |
| `previewDamage` | Signed HP, nonnegative damage | Exact new HP and standing / unconscious / dead |

| `resolveMorale` | Two d6; rating 2–12 | Sum; holds when sum ≤rating |

All numeric inputs and intermediate sums must fit JavaScript safe integers. Bounds in a calculator do not establish permission, life, fairness, valid equipment, safe shelter, a witness, or a world-time precondition. The human referee checks those circumstances; the existing authority separately validates any supported canonical action.
