# ARGO — Tempted Fates

*A living-world scenario, a warning in three voices, and a possible ending beyond the loom.*

> **Every world needs a vessel. Every vessel needs its crew. And every quest, its heroes.**  
> **Have an adventure.**

---

## The premise

There are rumors of things that can change the measure of a life. Some are weapons, some are stories, some are misunderstandings that have survived longer than the kingdoms that first repeated them. No public inventory can establish which artifacts exist in a given world, where they have traveled, who possesses them, or whether their supposed powers were ever real.

A traveler hears of a relic that might extend a mortal life. They seek the Fates, who are said to know all threads. The Fates are not quest-givers, merchants, or judges. They do not volunteer a path to the relic, confirm its existence, or promise that finding it will improve anything.

What follows is **one possible encounter and consequence**, not an unavoidable questline. The traveler may walk away at any point. A tale may remain only a tale. An artifact may never be found. Other players in other worlds may experience entirely different histories.

In the rarest possible telling, a seeker acquires more time than Fate can account for—and finds that escaping an ending is not the same thing as escaping the debt of being alive.

## The Fates: three voices, three jurisdictions

The three do not cooperate to produce convenient answers. Each speaks only for the part she holds. Their composure comes from being ancient, not cruel; their humor comes from mortals asking the wrong questions of the wrong sister.

| Fate | What is hers | Manner | What is not hers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Clotho — the Spinner** | Beginnings, the appearing of threads, possible starts | Curious, often amused, sometimes unexpectedly giddy | How long a thread lasts; what its bearer chooses |
| **Lachesis — the Measurer** | Length, allotment, order, and the accounting of time | Precise, dry, impatient with repeated questions | What should be done with the years; who deserves them |
| **Atropos — the Cutter** | The end of a thread | Spare, unhurried, dryly final | The story lived before the end; judgments of virtue or worth |

They do not meddle in what is not theirs. Nor do they disclose the unwritten story. Their knowledge of threads is not authority over the choices made along them.

**Important:** Their seeming ambiguity is not a deliberate trick. Each answer may be perfectly true and still fail to answer the question the traveler thought they asked.

# The scenario

## I. The loom

No door marks the entrance. No road is guaranteed to lead here twice. The sound arrives before the sight: wood, thread, a quiet measure, the closing of shears.

The traveler finds three figures working without ceremony. None rises to greet a hero. It is not clear whether the place is a chamber, a clearing, or the space between two thoughts.

**CLOTHO:** “The thread is spun.”

**LACHESIS:** “Its length is measured.”

**ATROPOS:** “But what becomes of it is another matter.”

**TRAVELER:** “And where does my thread end?”

**ATROPOS:** “Where it should. Never short, and never long.”

**TRAVELER:** “So you have already decided?”

**LACHESIS:** “Its length is measured.”

**CLOTHO:** “Its story remains unwritten.”

**TRAVELER:** “Then I can change my fate?”

**ATROPOS:** “You may change your story.”

A pause. The shears close on another thread.

**CLOTHO:** “Now, shall we begin? Best to arrive as foretold when tempting Fate.”

**TRAVELER:** “And if I arrive late?”

**ATROPOS:** “You will have arrived on time.”

The traveler may ask again. The answer will not become more useful simply because it is demanded more loudly.

## II. Of this world, or one like it?

The traveler speaks of an object rumored to be hidden beneath an old statue: a relic shown in a sketch, a song, perhaps a recording shared by a traveler from somewhere else. It is supposed to be here.

**ARGO:** “What is supposed to be where?”

**TRAVELER:** “The relic. I saw it in a recording.”

**ARGO:** “Of this world or another?”

**TRAVELER:** “This one.”

**ARGO:** “Or one like it?”

**TRAVELER:** “The same ruins. The same statue.”

**ARGO:** “A place may remain where it was. That does not require all things within it to do likewise.”

A relic might have been carried away, inherited, stolen, destroyed, misidentified—or never present. A recording describes the state of a world at some time, not the permanent inventory of every world that resembles it.

The Fates answer only what falls within their concerns.

**CLOTHO:**

> Different clothes of different strings  
> Hold different things.

**LACHESIS:**

> Worlds in state are not sealed fate.

**TRAVELER:** “Then the relic might still exist?”

**CLOTHO:** “It may have begun.”

**LACHESIS:** “It may have passed through many hands.”

**ATROPOS:** “Or none.”

**TRAVELER:** “Which is it?”

The loom continues.

## III. Some in fire, some in lore

A traveler seeking certainty hears a warning that offers none.

**CLOTHO:**

> Some were forged in fire,

**LACHESIS:**

> Some in lore,

**ATROPOS:**

> Both belong to others, and are not yours.

**TRAVELER:** “Not yet.”

**CLOTHO:** “Oh. An ambition.”

**LACHESIS:** “One you have spent considerable time pursuing.”

**ATROPOS:** “Perhaps longer than you possess.”

**TRAVELER:** “Is the artifact real?”

**CLOTHO:** “There are things that begin as stories.”

**LACHESIS:** “And stories that outlast the things they describe.”

**ATROPOS:** “Which one have you come seeking?”

The traveler is free to investigate, give up the search, or mistake a metaphor for a map. The world does not produce a relic merely because somebody wants there to be one.

An old verse—variously attributed to the Fates, a dead king, and a singer who had never left his village—circulates in competing versions:

> Whether it exists at all, or merely as metaphor,  
> The worthy may wield; they may pay a great cost.  
> Time wagered for power, too brief to be remembered.  
> Those found are few; debts cannot always be paid,  
> And regrets cannot be unmade.  
> A thread becomes longer, and yet short when reviewed.

No one can establish whether the verse was composed as a warning about a real object, or whether belief in the object was born from the warning.

## IV. The honest gift

If the world permits the discovery of a means to extend life, it is not a source of free years. The proposed extension must come from **time honestly and freely given by another living bearer of a thread**.

There is no entitlement to another's measure. No coerced gift counts as an honest one. Even an honest gift guarantees neither youth, happiness, fulfillment, nor a particular reward.

**TRAVELER:** “So the years can be given?”

**LACHESIS:** “Only those given honestly may be measured into another's keeping.”

**TRAVELER:** “And I receive all that was offered?”

**LACHESIS:** “You receive its measure. Nothing beyond it has been promised.”

**CLOTHO:** “A longer thread is not a new beginning.”

**ATROPOS:** “Nor a younger one.”

**TRAVELER:** “Then I will grow old?”

**LACHESIS:** “Years accumulate.”

**CLOTHO:** “Beginnings do not multiply because you wish to repeat them.”

**ATROPOS:** “Elders can become heavy with weary years.”

**TRAVELER:** “What if I live long enough to accomplish everything?”

**ATROPOS:** “Life is not the same as living.”

The Fates neither solicit the gift nor bless it. They can recognize a measure; they cannot decide whether it was worth giving or receiving.

### The choice remains open

The traveler can refuse the exchange, accept only what is freely offered, or continue searching for something else. No unseen narrator forces a price. The years are not taken off-screen to create a dramatic twist. Other characters have their own knowledge and interests. Their consent is part of the world's established history, not a convenient afterthought.

## V. The debt no measure settles

In one possible telling, people who love the traveler freely offer portions of their remaining years. The traveler accepts.

The gift is real. The lengthening is real. The years pass. The giver's absence, too, is real.

The traveler discovers that extra time does not turn a sacrifice into an achievement. The gift does not prove they deserved it, and the things they hoped to accomplish may prove harder than the asking.

At first, they avoid those who gave. They fear arriving empty-handed. They fear explaining what could have been worth more than the lives from which the years were taken. When asked whether they had enough, they hear an accusation that may never have been spoken.

There is no cosmic verdict waiting to make the arithmetic come out neatly.

**TRAVELER:** “They gave what they had. How can I face them with nothing to show for it?”

**LACHESIS:** “The measure was theirs to give.”

**TRAVELER:** “Were they right to give it?”

**CLOTHO:** “They chose.”

**TRAVELER:** “And was I right to take it?”

No sister answers immediately.

**TRAVELER:** “Even petty gods are not entitled to sacrifice. I am no god. What am I entitled to?”

**LACHESIS:** “What you have done is yours.”

**TRAVELER:** “Then who weighs it? Who is worthy to judge?”

**ATROPOS:** “Not I.”

**TRAVELER:** “Then who?”

**ATROPOS:** “You.”

It is not an accusation. It is the absence of an absolution she was never authorized to grant.

**ATROPOS:** “The givers accepted their cost. You must face yourself with what you accepted.”

The shears move again.

## VI. The thing that cannot be

The traveler has lived beyond what anyone expected. The record of the world contains gifts, transfers, witnesses, regret, and an extraordinary discrepancy. Something about the traveler's thread is no longer accounted for by the loom.

The Fates do not convene a tribunal. The traveler has not offended them. The first sign that anything is wrong is not anger.

It is delight.

Clotho stops spinning.

For the first time anyone remembers, she leans forward like a child who has discovered a color nobody has named.

**CLOTHO** *(giddy, clapping):* “Oh! Oh my! An a... ana...”

**TRAVELER:** “What?”

**CLOTHO:** “A... assem?... No...”

**LACHESIS:** “No thread has ever vanished from the loom.”

**CLOTHO:** “Inigm... no... Anononomemnie...”

**ATROPOS:** “What thread was ever there?”

For a moment the sisters regard the possibility of a thing for which none has a jurisdiction.

**CLOTHO:** “ANOMALY!”

She beams. The word has finally arrived.

And then she turns back to her work.

**TRAVELER:** “Vanished?”

Clotho draws a new strand between her fingers, regarding only its beginning.

**CLOTHO** *(to her work):* “No. Every thread has to begin.”

Lachesis counts another measure.

**LACHESIS** *(to her work):* “No. Every thread has to be measured.”

Atropos lowers her shears.

**ATROPOS** *(to her work):* “No thread has been lost.”

**TRAVELER:** “What happens to me?”

Nothing.

**TRAVELER:** “What am I now?”

The wood of the loom creaks. A strand is tightened. A cut is made.

**TRAVELER:** “You called me an anomaly.”

There is no refusal, no punishment, no dramatic silence performed for the traveler. Their attention has simply passed elsewhere. None acknowledges that an anomaly stands before them, because **there is no thread there for any of them to tend**.

The traveler had wanted to avoid an ending.

Now the traveler cannot even ask Fate when one will come.

## VII. Without even Fate

The world outside the loom remains. Other people still make choices. Promises remain recorded. Those who gave their time are not restored by the seeker's distress. No world authority declares the seeker evil or blessed, victorious or damned.

There may be witnesses. There may be debts, affection, anger, or forgiveness. There may be more years. Their true nature is not explained.

The Fates return to the ordinary work of beginnings, measures, and endings.

They are not concealing the solution.

They simply have nothing to say about a thread that is not there.

**He sought to outlast his fate. He was left without even Fate.**

# How this lives inside Argo

This scenario is not a mandatory boss encounter, a fixed set of dialog triggers, or a developer-declared relic location. It is a **possibility seeded into a living world's knowledge and causal state**.

### Story laws

1. **No inventory of certainty.** Relics can be genuine, lost, moved, destroyed, counterfeit, or entirely legendary. Similar worlds can contain different truths.
2. **Causal movement, not anti-walkthrough cheating.** An artifact moves only because a valid world event moves it. Argo does not relocate items simply because a player consults an online guide.
3. **The Fates have bounded authority.** Clotho speaks for beginnings, Lachesis for measure, Atropos for endings. They do not jointly impersonate omniscience, choose the hero's actions, or enforce moral judgments.
4. **A gift is a choice.** Time cannot be taken through coercion and then passed off as an honest gift. The system preserves who offered what, when, under what knowledge and conditions.
5. **A measure is not a reward.** Extra years do not imply youth, wisdom, exemption from regret, or automatic fulfillment.
6. **A rumor is not a prophecy.** The verse may refer to an artifact, a metaphor, an erroneous tradition, or a mixture of them. The game does not promise the seeker a particular discovery.
7. **The anomaly is not a loot mechanic to farm.** It is a rare, contingent metaphysical consequence—not a guaranteed branch a player can unlock by following a walkthrough.
8. **No explanatory epilogue.** After Clotho names the anomaly, the Fates return to their duties. Do not give them new dialogue interpreting its ontology. No one conclusively explains it on their behalf.
9. **No authored ending is owed.** The traveler may stop seeking, decline the gift, reconcile with those who helped, or never encounter the Fates at all. The world continues either way.

### Knowledge and state requirements

Argo maintains separate records of **world truth**, **historical events**, **source accounts**, **rumors**, and **what each actor believes**. For any relic it may track identity, last supported location, custodial transfers, conflicting sightings, claimed powers, and the scope of each witness's knowledge. An unknown property remains unknown to the traveler; it need not be unknown to the rules authority.

Time transfers, if possible, require an explicit transaction record with the identity and informed choice of each donor, the effective measure, and resulting world-state changes. The rules authority determines what can occur. The narrative layer describes only committed or properly qualified events.

The Fates are independent role-governed agents, not a scripted three-headed exposition device. Their dialogue can vary naturally while remaining within their responsibilities. Once the anomaly is named, the absence of further engagement is a **jurisdictional condition**, not a temporary mood or a quest prompt.

### The core paradox

The Fates are indispensable to all threads they know. The anomaly, by definition, cannot be admitted into the model they administer. To Clotho it is a thrilling first; to Lachesis, an impossibility of accounting; to Atropos, nothing requiring a cut.

Yet for the traveler, the event is not abstract. People gave years of their lives. Their choices remain. Their absence remains. The question of what the traveler owes them cannot be settled by the very beings who once measured every life.

**The Fates are indifferent. The world is not.**
